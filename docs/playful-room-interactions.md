# Basketball and bumper physics

Basketballs use a pull-back shot: direction and distance set the launch velocity, with a short initial arc preview and no hoop targeting. A tap opens controls; holding still for 300ms before dragging switches to carrying, or the explicit Move button does the same for touch/keyboard users. Aim arrows and a power slider provide a keyboard alternative. Escape or switching tools drops the held ball. Put back restores its original pickup.

Both local balls keep their landing positions for the current page session. The original agent-shot ball is reserved while a visitor has moved it; the spare can be played with independently. Floor, furniture and divider collisions use fixed substeps. Balls can move through the authored patio doorway or trigger the existing elevator tour, appear at the opposite cabin, and roll out. Different room floors share explicit, validated room coordinates. Other viewers receive the existing short-lived public ghost-ball channel, now with room metadata; this is not a globally owned inventory of permanently synchronized balls.

Car contact uses shared mass-based impulses. Parked cars can slide and settle; a blocked chain stops against walls/furniture. Server-controlled pedestrian nudges are bounded and retain real activity, owner, control lease and route destination. Held/boarding/seated agents and anonymous companion capsules remain protected. The DeLorean retains its height-based clearance above ground cars. The explicit local playground applies visual pedestrian offsets without sending avatar commands to production.

Validation: visitor-basketball tests cover manual direction/power, aim-dependent baskets at three frame rates, floor settlement, doorway mappings and room-aware relay rejection. Garage tests cover momentum, differing masses, blocked chains, hover clearance, lease preservation and authoritative pedestrian displacement. Browser checks included an actual pull-back release, moving a ball into the elevator and finding it downstairs, plus starting/driving/parking the Mini. Collision response itself was verified by simulation tests, not a timed browser crash recording.
