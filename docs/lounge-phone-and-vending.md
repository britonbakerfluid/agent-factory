# Lounge phone and vending interactions

The lounge conversation now lives on a modeled phone on the coffee table, beside the candle. Clicking it or using C moves the room camera to the phone's real screen; returning reverses that camera move. The former wall chat board is removed. The native scrollable conversation and composer fit the phone screen, with blue outgoing bubbles, slate incoming bubbles, sender names and timestamps. Existing history, ordered `chat_append` updates, authenticated sending, drafts, commands, and reconnect behavior use the same transport as before.

Front desk profiles, the physical team screen, phone header, and chat message portraits share one upper-body crop with a stepped circular mask. Real avatars come from the existing team roster or current agent identities. Unknown historical senders use an initial, not another person's avatar. Front desk contribution levels remain visible. Local development watching production remains read-only unless connected to its matching authenticated backend.

The front desk vending machine dispenses blue drink cans, golden chip bags, and coral chocolate bars from its pickup tray. Shelf stock, floor models, and carried pixel art share the same packaging. Repeated clicks queue snacks; gravity, angular momentum, floor friction, and capsule contacts produce a growing pile. At most 48 snacks are selected; a blocked pickup mouth waits instead of overlapping new objects. Settled objects sleep, hidden room views pause the simulation, and four instanced rendering batches bound drawing cost. Keyboard and touch share the projected minimum-44px target.

Nearby idle or manually controlled agents can claim one settled snack within .43 scene units, from the open front of the cabinet. Pickup removes the 3D floor instance immediately, wakes its supported neighbors, and lifts a matching flat pixel sprite into the agent's hand. The sprite follows the actual avatar frame, room/elevator reparenting, and avatar replacement. Work and other agent interactions clear the hand; otherwise a final sip or bite ends the carry after 21.2 seconds. A 12-second cooldown prevents an agent from collecting the whole pile at once. This is browser-local cosmetic state, reset on reload. It never changes agent routes or sends server commands. The local travel playground includes a “by snacks” shortcut for reviewing it safely.

Validation: 680 tests across 101 files and the client/server production build passed. New checks cover exclusive pickup ownership, matching 3D/pixel packaging, hand attachment through turns and room changes, busy-agent exclusions, cleanup/cooldown, object contacts, conservation, stable stacking, sleeping/waking, bounded repeated input, suspended-tab handling, frame-rate independence, and phone camera framing at desktop, mobile, and landscape sizes. Browser review checked mixed dispensing and carried pixel art at room/close-up scale, automatic pickup through the local movement controls, a 390×844 snack target measuring 44×44 with no horizontal overflow, visible floor contact, existing live chat history, composer focus, portrait consistency, and a 390×844 layout without message overflow. No chat message was sent during visual review. Physical-phone keyboard and frame-rate testing were not performed.
